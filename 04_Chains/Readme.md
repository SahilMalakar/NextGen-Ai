**5 Fascinating Space Facts**

1. **A “rainbow” of colors in the night sky** – When meteors burn up in Earth’s atmosphere they create a faint, multi‑colored glow called a *meteor trail spectrum*. The light splits into distinct wavelengths (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet), essentially a natural rainbow that can be captured with a spectrograph.

2. **The largest known star is a “hypergiant”** – **UY Scuti**, located about 9,500 light‑years away, has a radius ~1,700 times that of the Sun. If placed at the center of our Solar System, its surface would extend beyond the orbit of Saturn.

3. **Space is not completely empty – it’s filled with a faint “scent”** – Astronauts returning from EVA (extravehicular activity) have reported that their suits smell like seared steak, hot metal, or burnt garlic. The odor comes from high‑energy atomic oxygen reacting with the spacecraft’s exterior materials.     

4. **A day on Venus is longer than its year** – Venus rotates on its axis very slowly (a sidereal day ≈ 243 Earth days) but orbits the Sun faster (≈ 225 Earth days). Consequently, a Venusian solar day (sunrise to sunrise) lasts about 117 Earth days, while a Venusian year is shorter than a single Venusian day.     

5. **The Universe’s “cosmic web” is made of dark matter filaments** – Large‑scale surveys (e.g., Sloan Digital Sky Survey) have mapped galaxies that lie along vast, thread‑like structures. These filaments, composed primarily of invisible dark matter, stretch across hundreds of millions of light‑years, forming a web‑like skeleton that guides the formation of galaxy clusters.
